This project focused on designing a simple hand-operated nutcracker capable of cracking a macadamia nut using lever mechanics. The goal was to determine feasible dimensions for the tool so that the required cracking force could be generated using typical human grip strength. I developed a simple geometric model of the nutcracker. I found relevant input values, such as macadamia nut size, average cracking force, and maximum hand grip force through literature research. I then applied Lever mechanics to calculate the necessary handle length to achieve sufficient mechanical advantage, for an acceptable range of grip strengths. I evaluated my final design by discussing its usability, including required effort, and practicality for everyday use.

Through my research, I found values for the average load to crack a Macademia nut, 222.18 kg, typical size of a nut, 17.5 mm in diameter, and typical maximum grip strength for a man, 57.5 kg. By drawing a FBD of my potential design, and setting up a net moment equation, I was able to equate the ratios of the output force over the input force, to the distances of the opposite force from the fulcrum. By solving this ratio, I found the minimum distance at which the input force of 57.5 kg must be applied to produce an output force of 222.18 kg and crack the nut. The distance which I found was 33.81mm. 

![Sketch of initial Design]({{ "/assets/images/IMG_0091.jpeg" | relative_url }}){: .inline-image-r style="width: 200px"}

I made the decision to make the nutcracker a total length of 20cm, so that individuals with grip strengths all the way down to 9.72 kg, weaker than a typical 10 year old girl, could use the nutcracker. And also to make the handling of the object more practical. One hindrance of my nutcracker design is that it is only made to fit one size of nut, and would not be able to crack and smaller nuts, limiting the versatility of the object.
